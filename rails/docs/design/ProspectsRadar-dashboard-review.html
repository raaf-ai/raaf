Design review · Home dashboard

The first 10 seconds
after Lena logs in.

A walk-through of the production home dashboard — what a seller sees on arrival — with annotated notes against the Glass Morph system. This is a faithful reconstruction of HomeDashboard, not a redesign; the goal is to surface strengths, inconsistencies, and a short list of concrete improvements.

What the dashboard is trying to do

  • 1Reorient a returning seller in under 10 seconds: what changed, what needs me, what's hot.
  • 2Surface the top tasks and activities side-by-side so action and context live together.
  • 3Highlight hot prospects ready for outreach and newly discovered companies — the two things that drive the next call.

Annotations · 10 notes Numbered against the mock above
1
Hero tone is on-brand Voice aligns with the brand guide: "Good morning, Lena" + a specific count of what changed overnight. No cheerleader copy.
Keep · strength
2
Stats row has five peers, no hierarchy Five equally-weighted numbers. Open tasks and Ready to reach out are actionable; Avg score and Signals · 7 days are not. Consider lifting the two actionable stats to primary treatment.
Fix · medium
3
Tasks / Activities pairing works Action on the left, context on the right — a strong pattern for a sales dashboard. align-items: stretch keeps heights matched.
Keep · strength
4
Task filter pills need a count All / Mine / Account is the right axis, but filtering without seeing per-filter counts is guesswork. Add subscripts: Mine · 4.
Small · quick win
5
Activities filter is hidden behind a glyph "4 categories" reads like a label, not a control. It's the only significant filter surface and it's the weakest affordance on the page. Promote to the same pill-group pattern as Tasks.
Fix · medium
6
Section banner lacks a verb "Ready to reach out" is excellent copy — but the CTA next to it ("View all prospects") ducks the moment. Try Start outreach → for the hot list specifically.
Small · copy
7
Light-on-dark surface is a break from the system The lead-grid's #e2eaed background breaks the glass rhythm that every other tile maintains. If the intention is "this is scannable data, not interface," commit harder — but right now it looks like two designers shipped two different dashboards.
Decide · design system
8
Two consecutive light grids is a lot Ready-to-reach-out and New prospects use the same light card grid, back-to-back. On a dark dashboard, that's two big white blocks in a row. Alternate, or differentiate (e.g. a denser row layout for "new prospects").
Fix · medium
9
Widgets live below the fold of busy work Priority Targets and Warmth Ranking are arguably the most valuable intelligence on the dashboard but sit fourth from the top. Consider exposing Priority Targets above the Tasks/Activities grid for power users, behind a preference.
Explore · layout
10
Background signals closes the page well Three zoomed-out summaries after the tactical work — a natural "anything else I should know?" coda. Good voice ("auto-summarized" sets correct expectations).
Keep · strength
Strengths
  • VoiceHero + section copy follows the brand guide. Specific counts, active verbs, no jargon.
  • Tasks ↔ ActivitiesPairing action with context is the right spine for a sales dashboard.
  • Glass Morph executed wellHero gradient, glass tiles, and section tiles read as one system.
  • Score rampApplied consistently from stat cards to lead cards to priority list.
Tensions
  • Flat hierarchyStats row, tasks, activities, hot leads, new leads — all equally loud. Nothing says "start here."
  • Light-on-dark inversionLead-grid's light surface breaks the system twice in a row. Either commit to a pattern or retreat.
  • Filter affordances are unevenTasks use pill filters; Activities use a quiet text trigger. Same job, different UI.
  • Vertical lengthNine stacked sections. On a 1080 screen, most users never see Priority Targets or Warmth Ranking.
Gaps
  • No "today vs. earlier"Everything is presented as equally fresh. The dashboard doesn't distinguish overnight signals from week-old ones.
  • No empty/loading states hereEmpty states exist as components but the composition assumes populated data. Worth a dedicated review pass.
  • No dismiss / snooze on activities184 activities with no way to acknowledge and move past. Rows feel read-only.
  • No personalizationSame layout for everyone — no reorder, no section show/hide, no "pin to top."

Recommendations

Ranked by impact · estimated effort
  1. Small
    Promote the two actionable stats Make Open tasks and Ready to reach out visually primary in the hero stats row — larger value, tinted background, a live "start" link. Demote avg score and signal count to a secondary micro-row underneath.
  2. Small
    Unify filter affordances Use the same pill-group pattern for task filters and activity category filters. Include per-filter counts in both. It's the same ergonomics, it should look the same.
  3. Medium
    Pick a lane on the lead grid surface Three options, in order of preference: (a) return lead cards to the dark glass surface for system consistency; (b) reserve the light surface for New prospects only as a visual signal ("cold, inbound"), and keep Ready to reach out on dark to match its urgency; (c) commit to light for all tabular prospect data and restyle the rest of the system around that rule.
  4. Medium
    Add a "since last login" header band A thin 32px strip above the hero: Since Monday 14:02: 12 new signals · 4 hot leads · 2 tasks became overdue. This is the single most useful first glance for a returning user and costs nothing to compute.
  5. Medium
    Let users dismiss or snooze an activity row Add a hover-revealed × Dismiss and ⏰ Snooze 24h on each activity row. Without this, the list is noise-generating, not noise-reducing.
  6. Large
    Introduce a "Focus mode" layout preference Add an account setting that collapses the dashboard to just Priority Targets + Tasks. Power users who already trust the scoring want to skip past the briefing and go straight to work. Preserve the current full layout as the default.
  7. Large
    Review the empty and loading compositions A separate walk-through with real-world variations — new account (no data), long-absent user (stale data), Freshsales sync in progress (partial data). The current reconstruction assumes an ideal populated state; the corner cases deserve the same scrutiny.
